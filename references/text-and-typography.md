# Thumbnail Text and Typography

## Text is optional

First ask:

**Can the image communicate the value in one glance?**

If yes, text may be unnecessary.

## When text helps

Text is especially useful when the image alone cannot communicate:

- the angle
- the result
- a key number
- the contradiction
- the emotional framing
- the unanswered question

## Length

Use a short hook, commonly 1–5 words depending on context.

Do not treat word counts as hard laws. Meaning, font size, language, and composition matter.

## Typography

Prefer:

- highly legible sans-serif styles
- strong weight
- sufficient line spacing
- strong contrast
- limited font count

Use no more than 1–2 primary fonts in most branded systems.

## Placement

Avoid:

- covering important faces
- bottom-right UI conflicts
- tiny corners
- complex backgrounds behind letters

Use negative space deliberately.

## Contrast

Evaluate both:

- text/background contrast
- text/subject separation

Contrast should be checked at mobile size, not only at full resolution.

## One-variable typography tests

When testing text:

- keep composition stable
- change wording OR font OR color OR position
- record the exact change

This makes results interpretable.
